We use time-resolved tomographic imaging to follow solid spheres through high-speed flow in a round pipe, across the sudden area change where the fluid reorganises and the solids cannot. Measurements of this kind were considered unattainable only a few years ago.

The enabling technique is refractive index matching. The working fluid, a concentrated aqueous sodium iodide solution, has the same refractive index as the acrylic pipe and the acrylic spheres, so light passes through the curved wall and through the particles undistorted. Four high-speed cameras then see the whole volume at once, and the tracks are reconstructed with the Shake-The-Box algorithm.

How solids travel through high-speed pipe flow matters wherever pipelines carry more than one phase: oil and gas lines where blockage and wear are a concern, chemical plant where catalyst is circulated, and water distribution systems.
