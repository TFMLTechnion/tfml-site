01 / Research

All research ↗

Fast events.
Open questions.

Two lines of work run through the lab: compressible flows and shock waves, and multiphase flows made visible by refractive index matching. Both rest on time-resolved measurements of events that are over in less than a second.

    An index-matched sphere is invisible to our cameras. We locate it from the flow it disturbs and measure the drag and lift on it as it rises.

02 / A measurement

A rising sphere,
frame by frame.

A buoyant sphere does not rise in a straight line. The vortices it sheds pull on it, and its path wanders.

These ten snapshots from a single experiment follow the wake over 220 milliseconds: two vortex threads form, stretch and break into rings, and the drag on the sphere rises and falls with them.

The people behind
the measurements.

The lab is led by Omri Ram, assistant professor in the Faculty of Mechanical Engineering, who trained in shock-wave physics at Ben-Gurion University and in experimental fluid mechanics at Johns Hopkins University.
