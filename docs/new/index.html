Fluid mechanics at the speed of change

We are a group of experimentalists who study what fluids do in the instants when everything changes: when a shock wave sweeps through a duct, when a bubble is born on a hot wall, when a body tears free of its own wake.

These moments last microseconds to milliseconds and decide how engines breathe, how blasts are softened, how particles travel through pipelines and how oil spills spread. We build the instruments that can see them, cameras that look a hundred thousand times a second and tanks in which solid bodies become invisible, and we measure the forces at work.

Two hundred milliseconds, frame by frame

Our cameras record four thousand eight hundred pictures a second from four directions at once. Stacked together they show, for the first time, how the vortices a rising sphere leaves behind push it off course, and how it speeds up the moment they let go.

Principal investigator

Omri Ram

Omri Ram is an assistant professor in the Faculty of Mechanical Engineering at the Technion, where he founded the Transient Fluid Mechanics Laboratory in 2021. He trained in shock-wave physics at Ben-Gurion University and, as a Fulbright postdoctoral fellow at Johns Hopkins University, studied how cavitation begins and how oil and water separate after a spill. He is a Zuckerman Faculty Scholar and holds a Career Advancement Chair at the Technion, which has twice recognised his teaching with a commendation for excellence.
