Research / Multiphase and index-matched flows

Buoyancy-driven spheres rising in quiescent fluid

Time-resolved tomographic PIV around freely rising spheres resolves the wake, the pressure field and the transient loads that make the sphere oscillate.

Buoyancy-driven bodies in a viscous fluid experience complex fluid–structure interactions. In the Reynolds number regime of this motion, unsteady vortex shedding produces transient loads on the body, and the resulting oscillatory trajectory has been studied extensively with numerical simulations. Experimental studies of the flow field around such bodies are sparse: most of the literature concentrates on drag measurements of freely falling bodies rather than the flow around them. Yet the dynamics of free-falling and free-rising bodies matter in many natural and industrial processes, from seeds falling from a tree and oil droplets rising from an underwater well to solids settling in a thickening tank and agglomerates moving in chemical reactors.

Typically, image tracking and particle image velocimetry (PIV) are combined to record the motion of the body and the flow around it, using two-dimensional, stereo or tomographic PIV. Because these studies rarely capture the whole three-dimensional field around the body, there are almost no direct measurements of the coupled fluid–structure interaction.

We use refractive-index-matched, time-resolved tomographic PIV with four high-speed cameras to capture the flow around freely rising spheres of different diameters at terminal velocity. Shake-The-Box particle tracking gives the Lagrangian velocity fields, from which the instantaneous pressure field around the sphere is estimated by three-dimensional omni-directional integration of the material acceleration. With both the pressure and velocity fields in hand, the transient loading and the dynamics of the rising body can be investigated in great detail.
